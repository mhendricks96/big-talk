If you've ever felt invisible, overwhelming, or somehow responsible for your parents' emotional wellbeing, this book is likely to feel like someone finally put language to your experience.

Gibson writes clearly and without jargon. She describes emotionally immature parents not as monsters, but as people who — for whatever reason — were never able to tolerate depth, closeness, or their children's authentic needs. The result for adult children is often a deep, quiet loneliness, a tendency to over-function, and a nagging sense that something is wrong with *them* rather than with the dynamic they grew up in.

What I find most clinically useful is how the book helps readers identify their own "role" in the family system — whether they became caretakers, entertainers, or simply learned to disappear. Gibson also offers very practical guidance for how to stop internalizing that old dynamic and start relating to yourself differently.
