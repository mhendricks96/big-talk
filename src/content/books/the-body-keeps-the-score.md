This is one of the books I return to most often — both personally and in my work with clients. Van der Kolk writes with a rare combination of clinical precision and deep compassion, and the result is a book that fundamentally changed how the mental health field understands trauma.

The central argument is deceptively simple: trauma is not just a psychological wound. It lives in the body. The nervous system holds the imprint of overwhelming experiences long after the mind has tried to move on. This is why "just talking about it" often isn't enough — and why so many trauma survivors feel stuck even after years of traditional talk therapy.
